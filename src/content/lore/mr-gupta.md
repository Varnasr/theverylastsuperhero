The reason the journey is also a work trip: a meeting to discuss climate change
solutions for the city.

He is *Mr* Gupta in the book, and the archive had him wrong — filed as a doctor,
and illustrated as a woman in a clinic. Both were inventions of the cataloguer.

The meeting does not happen the way it was meant to. Sumati remembers it
afterwards as one of the loose threads: *we were going to meet with a Mr Gupta,
but Papa met a Mr Joshi.* Later, the search runs the two names together —
cross-reference either a Mr Gupta or a Mr Joshi or both — which is what you do
with names when you no longer know which one mattered.

By the end he is not a colleague. He is on a list.

> We should also improve our relationships with Mr Gupta and the Thakurs.
>
> — Rohan

> We need to address Mr Gupta and the Thakurs as well. They're still out there,
> causing damage.

Both sentences are in the book, and the distance between them is the argument
the family is having: whether the people running the plants are partners you
bring round, or a problem you name.
