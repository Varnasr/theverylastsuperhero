During that time, science was under siege in India.

Public universities were pushed to adopt a curriculum based on so-called
*Indigenous Knowledge Systems* — a blend of astrology, myth and nationalist
revisionism. Teacher training programmes became tools of indoctrination. Anyone
who questioned the state's version of science was labelled anti-national.

> They taught that flying chariots were ancient aerospace. It wasn't just lies.
> It was a full retreat from reason.

The name is the trick. There is real knowledge held in oral and written
traditions, and there are real practices worth recovering — which is exactly why
the label was worth stealing. Wrapping revisionism in heritage meant that
objecting to the revisionism could be made to look like contempt for the heritage.

It is worth reading this entry against [the salt lamps](/lore/salt-lamps). The
lamps are not a claim that the ancients had it all along. They are halite,
[hysterons](/lore/hysterons), compression tolerances and a great deal of failed
prototyping — the discipline the curriculum was built to replace.

Educational institutions were repurposed to erode critical thinking. It worked.
That is the quiet horror underneath the rest of the book: by the time the
archives failed, a generation had already been trained not to notice.
