The sun scorches the dusty streets. Fields that were once lush-green bear the
brunt of it, turned to arid patches with cracks running deep.

### Environmental snapshot — Kishanganj sector, 2074

| | |
| --- | --- |
| Average surface temperature | 55.6°C |
| Average max indoor temp (non-cooled) | 49.1°C |
| Precipitation, annual | 61% below 2020 baseline |
| Child malnutrition | 63% (est.) |
| Primary food source | rations via damaged dispenser grid; black-market hydration pods |
| Predominant climate risks | fatal heatstroke, aquifer collapse, airborne fungal spores, PM2.5: 312 |
| Local designation | Inadaptable zone (Category 2) |
| Population change since 2060 | −43% |

Emergency cooling shelters are mandatory for survival.

*Inadaptable* is an administrative word, and it is doing a great deal of work.
It does not mean uninhabited. Roughly half the people are still there, running
a tea stall at the end of the platform and arguing about the bus timetable at
49°C indoors.

What the designation actually records is that the state has stopped planning for
them.

### What is underneath it

> This used to be a coordination node. That's why the comms lines still hum
> underground. Superheroes came through here once, headed to rescue missions that
> failed.
>
> — [Nandini](/lore/nandini-maa)

Most of what still works in Kishanganj was salvaged: helmets no longer worn,
boots that used to hover, devices that once scanned loyalty. The infrastructure
of the hero era, stripped for parts and put to use by the people it was supposed
to save.

> The heat isn't just in the air. It's in the memory of the ground.
>
> — Station-side proverb, Kishanganj
