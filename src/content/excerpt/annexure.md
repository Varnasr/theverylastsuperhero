*Why Cities Drown and Children Burn: The Climate Science Behind “The Very Last Superhero”*

By 2075, India will be hotter, wetter, and more unequal. This novel's heat spikes, submerged neighbourhoods, and cooling uniforms are grounded in accurate projections, notably a 2024 systematic review on Indian heatwaves (Heliyon) and a 2025 NBER Working Paper on urban flood adaptation.

### 1. Heat and Human Limits: What 50°C Means

The 2024 Heliyon review projects 30–70 days annually above 40°C in Indian cities by mid-century. Parts of central and northern India will routinely exceed 50°C. Wet-bulb temperatures crossing 31°C render outdoor work or school unsafe. These are no longer anomalies—they are the baseline for summer.

Children like Sumati need heat-regulating school uniforms. Once reserved for soldiers or relief workers, cooling suits have become middle-class necessities. But access is skewed. In the novel, survival hinges not on adaptation alone but on affordability.

### 2. Drowning Cities: Flood Impact and Unequal Adaptation

The 2025 Gandhi et al. study shows that floods reduce urban economic activity by 7.4% in Indian cities, with some coastal regions facing post-flood losses as high as 16%. Poorer areas suffer more, both in terms of impact and recovery.

In the novel, Kishanganj is one such “inadaptable” zone—marked by aquifer collapse, ration breakdowns, and institutional decay. These conditions mirror the study’s findings on infrastructure neglect and state fragility.

Cities exposed to frequent flooding do adapt—but unevenly. Wealthier metros invest in resilient infrastructure, while peripheral towns like Ramnagar don’t. Learning is not universal.

### 3. Migration Is Not a Safety Net

The same study shows that migration out of flood-prone zones in India is rare, even after repeated disasters. Financial, social, and policy constraints trap families in harm’s way.

Sumati’s parents don’t move. They rewire their home, ration energy, and adapt incrementally. This is not agency. It is entrapment mistaken for resilience.

### 4. When Memory Becomes Infrastructure

In the novel, crystalline salt lamps act as data beacons—conductive, sentient, archival. The science here is speculative but not fantastical: conductive minerals and innovative materials are under active study.

This metaphor contrasts with real infrastructure failure. Gandhi et al. show that dams offer protection mostly in high-income cities. In India, dams often fail in poor towns. The novel’s “Treasure Room” reclaims memory where the state has been unable to record.

### 5. Adaptation Is Unequal—But Not Impossible

Adaptation is already happening, but uneven and contingent on money, memory, and continuity. Cooling suits, AI bureaucracies, rationed electricity, and closed train stations are not warnings—they are adaptations already underway.

*This annexure is not speculative fiction. If it feels exaggerated, it’s only because the metrics have yet to catch up with the next emergency.*
