---
term: "Video terminal"
section: "Surveillance tools and public infrastructure"
order: 31
---
The video terminal is the 2075 successor to 20th-century televisions and early bright screens. It is a flat, wall-mounted or embedded display unit that receives state-sanctioned news, educational content, biometric-verified video calls and commercial programming. Unlike traditional TVs, video terminals are always connected to centralised information networks and operate under content governance protocols. Most homes are issued one as part of ration entitlements.

They look passive, but they often include monitoring tools such as eye-tracking, silence timers and voice pattern logging, ostensibly for interactivity and frequently used for behavioural profiling. In The Very Last Superhero, Sumati’s household terminal flickers through news updates, AI announcements and curated broadcasts about the climate corps legacy. Meera turns it off regularly, aware of its dual role as informer and informant.

Terminals cannot be turned off permanently. When not in use they enter “ambient mode” and display government-approved visualisations or public health messaging. In public spaces they act like communal mirrors, showing what the system wants the community to see. They serve as windows to the outside world, and they also limit what that world looks like. In a media-saturated society the video terminal functions as architecture that shapes what people see, and it offers no real entertainment.
