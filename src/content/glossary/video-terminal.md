---
term: "Video Terminal"
section: "Surveillance Tools and Public Infrastructure"
order: 31
---
The 2075 successor to 20th-century televisions and early bright screens, the video terminal is a flat, wallmounted or embedded display unit for receiving state-sanctioned news, educational content, biometricverified video calls, and commercial programming. Unlike traditional TVs, video terminals are always connected to centralised information networks and operate under content governance protocols. Most homes are issued one as part of ration entitlements.

While they appear passive, these terminals often include passive monitoring tools—eye-tracking, silence timers, and voice pattern logging—ostensibly for interactivity but frequently used for behavioural profiling. In The Very Last Superhero, Sumati’s household terminal flickers through news updates, AI announcements, and curated broadcasts about the climate corps legacy. Meera turns it off regularly, aware of its dual role as informer and informant.

Terminals cannot be turned off permanently; they enter “ambient mode” when not used, displaying government-approved visualisations or public health messaging. In public spaces, terminals act like communal mirrors, showing what the system wants the community to see. While they serve as windows to the outside world, they also limit what that world looks like. In a media-saturated society, the video terminal is not entertainment—it’s architecture.
