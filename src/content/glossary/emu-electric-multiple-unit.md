EMU (Electric Multiple Unit) trains were initially developed for short-distance railway routes. They run on electrified tracks without a separate locomotive, which makes urban and semi-urban transit efficient. By 2075, in The Very Last Superhero, the EMU has become a resilient relic of a crumbling grid and still operates across specific routes despite the collapse of centralised control systems.

These modified EMUs run partly on scavenged solar power, onboard kinetic storage and makeshift capacitor banks, cobbled together by railway engineers, tinkerers and unauthorised mechanics who form a loose network of “track guardians.” The trains were once symbols of modern India’s public transport efficiency. They are now patched-up, graffiti-covered vessels with stripped interiors, and they carry a mix of humans, animals, contraband and lost children.

Railway signs are mostly in disrepair, so passengers rely on oral updates, rhythmic stops and exterior heat indicators, which are colour-shifting thermal patches or coded paint, to judge how long a carriage has been in the sun and whether it is safe to enter. With no functioning central control, some EMUs are driven manually or halted by Resistance-affiliated railway staff who know which lines are secure. The EMU represents movement against the odds: fragmented and slow but still running, a lifeline reclaimed from the state by the people.
