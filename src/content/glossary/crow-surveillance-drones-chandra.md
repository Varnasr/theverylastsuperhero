Rumoured to be bio-drones deployed during the late superhero era, crow surveillance drones were designed for stealth, mobility, and emotional invisibility. Black, organic-looking, and intelligent, they could mimic flocking patterns and auditory signals. In the story, Chandra, the large crow that follows Sumati, is never confirmed to be mechanical or natural. It behaves with eerie precision: appearing at key narrative moments, retrieving lost items, observing from above, and sometimes vanishing for days.

Chandra carries Sumati’s lost ID card, leads her to shelter, and witnesses several Resistance rituals. This ambiguity makes Chandra powerful—a character and a symbol. It represents memory that refuses to die, and surveillance that may be repurposed. Resistance members used such drones to archive footage, deliver micro-messages, and mislead tracking systems. In Indian folklore, crows are messengers, ancestors, and witnesses—these layers deepen Chandra’s narrative weight.

The idea of surveillance taking on a protective rather than punitive role is rare in dystopian fiction, but Chandra embodies that possibility. It is not domesticated. It is not understood. But it is loyal—and that loyalty matters more than whether it was ever coded.
