Crow surveillance drones are rumoured to be bio-drones deployed during the late superhero era. They were designed for stealth, mobility and emotional invisibility. Black, organic-looking and intelligent, they could mimic flocking patterns and auditory signals. In the story, Chandra, the large crow that follows Sumati, is never confirmed to be mechanical or natural. It behaves with eerie precision: it appears at key moments in the narrative, retrieves lost items, watches from above and sometimes vanishes for days.

Chandra carries Sumati’s lost ID card, leads her to shelter and witnesses several Resistance rituals. This ambiguity makes Chandra powerful as a character and as a symbol. It represents memory that refuses to die and surveillance that may be repurposed. Resistance members used such drones to archive footage, deliver micro-messages and mislead tracking systems. In Indian folklore crows are messengers, ancestors and witnesses, and these layers deepen Chandra’s narrative weight.

Surveillance that plays a protective role is rare in dystopian fiction, and Chandra embodies that possibility. It is wild and unexplained, and it is loyal. That loyalty matters more than whether anyone ever coded it.
