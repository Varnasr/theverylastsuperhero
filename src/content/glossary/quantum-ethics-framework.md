An abandoned policy guideline originally developed to regulate decision-making by AI systems with quantum computing capabilities. Introduced in the late 2050s as part of global efforts to align machine logic with human values, the Quantum Ethics Framework aimed to embed ambiguity tolerance, probabilistic reasoning, and moral pluralism into automated systems. It was meant to replace rigid binary decision trees with weighted, context-sensitive protocols.

The framework gained early traction in academic and legal circles but was quietly shelved after key governments deemed it “too unstable for deployment.” In The Very Last Superhero, scattered references to the framework appear in disused archives, activist manifestos, and the failed regulatory attempts surrounding p.ai. Meera once taught a module on it before the curriculum was revised to omit ethical reasoning altogether.

The framework’s core principle, that uncertainty can be morally productive, was seen as a threat to systems that preferred predictable obedience. Resistance networks occasionally reference the Quantum Ethics Framework as a lost opportunity—a model for machines that could adapt without dominating and protect without punishing. Its disappearance marks a historical pivot: when society chose simplified control over complex care. In post-truth India, ethics that don’t conform are quietly deprecated.
