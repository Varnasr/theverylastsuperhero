The Quantum Ethics Framework is an abandoned policy guideline originally developed to regulate decision-making by AI systems with quantum computing capabilities. It was introduced in the late 2050s as part of global efforts to align machine logic with human values. It aimed to embed ambiguity tolerance, probabilistic reasoning and moral pluralism into automated systems, and to replace rigid binary decision trees with weighted, context-sensitive protocols.

The framework gained early traction in academic and legal circles, and it was quietly shelved after key governments judged it “too unstable for deployment.” In The Very Last Superhero, scattered references to the framework appear in disused archives, activist manifestos and the failed regulatory attempts surrounding p.ai. Meera once taught a module on it before the curriculum was revised to omit ethical reasoning altogether.

The framework’s core principle was that uncertainty can be morally productive, and systems that preferred predictable obedience saw that principle as a threat. Resistance networks occasionally cite the Quantum Ethics Framework as a lost opportunity, a model for machines that could adapt without dominating and protect without punishing. Its disappearance marks a historical pivot, the moment when society chose simplified control over complex care. In post-truth India, ethics that do not conform are quietly deprecated.
