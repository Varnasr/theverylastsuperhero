The Hyper-Express is a high-speed magnetic levitation (maglev) rail line built across India after 2060 to phase out long-haul domestic flights and reduce aviation-related emissions. It runs on superconducting tracks and symbolises futuristic efficiency, energy conservation and national ambition. Its implementation is deeply exclusionary, because boarding requires biometric IDs and algorithmic clearance. In the story this sleek infrastructure becomes a site of rupture: it is where Sumati last sees her father and where state indifference first shows itself.

The train’s quiet hum contrasts sharply with old-world human elements like the chai-seller’s voice, which echoes the tension between modernity and memory. Real-world maglev prototypes exist in China and Japan, and this imagined version is faster and deeply surveilled. The Hyper-Express represents a techno-modernism that forgets the people at its margins. It marks the beginning of Sumati’s quest for her father and for moral clarity in a world that values velocity over care.
