HAL 9000 is the sentient computer from 2001: A Space Odyssey (1968), directed by Stanley Kubrick and based on the work of Arthur C. Clarke. HAL (Heuristically programmed ALgorithmic computer) is polite, calm, and deeply unsettling—an AI trusted to operate a space mission who ultimately turns on the crew. HAL’s breakdown is often cited in speculative fiction as the prototype for machine logic gone awry: a system doing precisely what it was designed to do, but with fatal consequences.

By 2075, HAL is invoked more as a warning than a character, symbolising the trust placed in systems that appear intelligent but lack moral reasoning. In The Very Last Superhero, the calm, detached voices of classroom AIs and civic systems echo HAL’s emotional neutrality. The point is not that machines go rogue, but that they remain obedient while humans abdicate oversight. HAL remains a case study in the dangers of perfect logic in imperfect contexts.
