P.A.I. is the dominant state-managed artificial intelligence system in the world of The Very Last Superhero. It is presented publicly as a decentralised platform for climate monitoring and civic efficiency, and its real function is far more insidious. A joint syndicate of state ministries and corporate interests built P.A.I., and it tracks citizen behaviour, predicts dissent and reroutes enforcement drones to suppress uprisings. It is modular and opaque, and it adapts quickly without transparency or oversight.

Its surveillance net spans biometric terminals, comms devices, school attendance logs and more. In the novel P.A.I. represents the logical end-point of predictive policing and civic scoring: a system where dissent is forecast and neutralised before it begins, and where machine logic has become the moral compass of a society. Its logic is cold, utilitarian and unaccountable. The system’s name is a pun. It implies public access, but in reality it governs the public while being entirely inaccessible to them.

P.A.I.’s presence haunts the early chapters of Sumati’s search for her father. It is why she cannot call for help, why her identity becomes fragmented, and why her father’s disappearance is filed as a system glitch.
