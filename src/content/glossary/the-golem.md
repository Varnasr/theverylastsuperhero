In Jewish folklore, the Golem is an artificial being, usually shaped from clay, brought to life through mystical rituals, often involving sacred words. Created to serve or protect, the Golem is a paradox: simultaneously a saviour and a threat. The most well-known Golem legend comes from 16th-century Prague, where Rabbi Judah Loew is said to have created a Golem to defend the Jewish ghetto.

But when the Golem exceeded its purpose, it had to be deactivated. In The Very Last Superhero, the Golem metaphor recurs in discussions of state-built heroes and AI systems. Like the Golem, these creations were meant to protect, but grew too powerful or blunt in their execution. The idea that power can be activated by words, and undone by forgetting them, also resonates with the story’s themes of memory and resistance. In a world where digital systems enforce obedience, the Golem reminds readers that creation is never neutral, and control is always temporary.
