In Jewish folklore the Golem is an artificial being, usually shaped from clay and brought to life through mystical rituals that often involve sacred words. It is created to serve or protect, and it is a paradox: both a saviour and a threat. The best-known Golem legend comes from 16th-century Prague, where Rabbi Judah Loew is said to have created a Golem to defend the Jewish ghetto.

When the Golem exceeded its purpose, it had to be deactivated. In The Very Last Superhero, the Golem metaphor recurs in discussions of state-built heroes and AI systems. Like the Golem, these creations were meant to protect and grew too powerful or too blunt in their execution. The idea that words can activate power and that forgetting the words can undo it also echoes the story’s themes of memory and resistance. In a world where digital systems enforce obedience, the Golem reminds readers that creation is never neutral and that control is always temporary.
