---
term: "Audible enclaves"
section: "Surveillance tools and public infrastructure"
order: 15
---
Audible enclaves are a sound technology that lets one person in a shared space hear a message while everyone around them hears nothing. It needs no headphones and no raised volume. The first versions used intersecting ultrasonic beams to create a tight “listening bubble” that follows the intended recipient. In the Resistance’s adaptation, enclave acoustics were built into salt lamp infrastructure to carry memory, warnings and archived sound across collapsing systems.

In the novel a lamp sometimes speaks to a single person and to nobody else in the room. People often mistake this for intuition or hallucination, but the signal is real and it arrives only when the time is right. The technology is based on real research, developed initially by researchers at Pennsylvania State University in 2024. Their system uses intersecting ultrasonic beams to create a private listening bubble around an individual. The effect works in real time, moves with the person and cannot be detected by anyone else in the same environment.
