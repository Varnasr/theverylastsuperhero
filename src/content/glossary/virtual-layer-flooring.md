term: "Virtual Layer Flooring"
section: "Core Technologies and Systems"
order: 2
---
A modular sensory surface installed on high-speed transit systems like the Hyper-Express, the virtual layer of flooring provides interactive feedback underfoot. Developed initially as an accessibility feature, it uses pressure-sensitive smart polymers embedded with micro projectors to emit visual cues—light paths, alerts, or emergency signals—based on movement, foot pressure, or programmed schedules. In commercial use by 2068, this flooring will be standard in state-run maglev and intercity transport.

Passengers walking across it leave transient trails of soft-colored light, often coded for visibility or status (e.g., red trails for security personnel, blue for standard passengers). The system syncs with train AI to update directions during boarding, redirect foot traffic, or identify anomalies in gait that might indicate distress. Rohan Kapoor walks across it shortly after being separated from Sumati, leaving a “blue blur of soft light”—a visual trace that symbolises both presence and absence.

While it enhances safety and navigation, it is also used by surveillance systems to map passenger movement, feed behavioural data into predictive models, and flag deviations from assigned zones. Like many technologies in The Very Last Superhero, the virtual floor is convenient and a control tool, quietly shaping how people move through a monitored space.
