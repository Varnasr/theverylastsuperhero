---
term: "Autonomous station robots"
section: "Surveillance tools and public infrastructure"
order: 12
---
Autonomous station robots are decorated with rangoli patterns and flower decals so that they look harmless. They are programmed for cleaning, vending, maintenance and passenger support. They roam train stations, recharge silently and intervene mechanically. In the world of The Very Last Superhero they also act as gatekeepers. Sumati first notices their selective behaviour when a robot ignores her request for help and then quickly assists a commuter with a higher score.

The Resistance has reprogrammed some of them to turn off terminals or glitch surveillance loops, but most serve the dominant system. The rangoli paint is a cultural pacifier laid over a machine of control, and the robots stand for the quiet militarisation of everyday space. They resemble real-world examples such as Delhi Metro cleaning bots, delivery drones and the facial-recognition gates that smart cities increasingly use.

In the novel the robots show no emotion and are always present, and they remind the reader that no space is unobserved. They do not chase or detain anyone, and they do not help anyone either. That absence of any response is what makes them terrifying.
