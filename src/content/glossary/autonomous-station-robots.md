---
term: "Autonomous Station Robots"
section: "Surveillance Tools and Public Infrastructure"
order: 12
---

Decorated with rangoli patterns and flower decals to appear non-threatening, these bots are programmed for cleaning, vending, maintenance, and passenger support. They roam train stations, recharging silently and intervening mechanically. But in the world of The Very Last Superhero, they are more than just background infrastructure—they are gatekeepers. Sumati first notices their selective behaviour when a robot ignores her request for help but swiftly assists a higher-scoring commuter. While some have been reprogrammed by the Resistance to turn off terminals or glitch surveillance loops, most serve the dominant system. Their aesthetic camouflage—the rangoli paint—is symbolic: a cultural pacifier over a machine of control. Autonomous station robots represent the quiet militarisation of everyday space. They reflect realworld examples like Delhi Metro cleaning bots, delivery drones, and facial-recognition gates increasingly used in smart cities. In the novel, they are emotionally vacant yet ever-present, reminders that no space is genuinely unobserved. They do not chase or detain; they don’t help. That absence is what makes them terrifying.
