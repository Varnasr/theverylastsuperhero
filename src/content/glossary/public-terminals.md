term: "Public terminals"
section: "Surveillance tools and public infrastructure"
order: 11
---
Public terminals were designed as a digital commons for accessing civic services. They are now biometric-controlled interfaces that exclude the undocumented and the un-scored. In 2075 these terminals are ubiquitous, found in train stations, post offices, food distribution points and libraries. Their screens offer state-approved news, weather forecasts, ration updates and surveillance alerts. For Sumati they function as walls, and she cannot log in, request help or confirm her identity.

Her father’s disappearance goes unrecorded because she cannot access the system. The novel’s terminals are a recurring motif, and they show how the state’s architecture no longer recognises people outside its scoring logic. They were designed during the IKS “tech-nationalism” boom and introduced with grand rhetoric about access and equity. Over time they became tools of quiet erasure. In real life, Aadhaar-authenticated services offer a parallel, since access depends on fingerprint scans or OTPs, which often fail at critical moments.

In the story the public terminals work perfectly, and that is the problem. Their design ensures that the people most in need are likely to be locked out.
