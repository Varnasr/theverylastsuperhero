Cooling suits are thermoregulating garments made with nanomaterials and graphene composites. They are designed to protect wearers from extreme temperatures above 50°C, and they use active cooling systems first developed for military and emergency personnel. In 2075, urban middle-class people wear them daily because climate extremes have become the norm. Schools issue reduced-function versions, called Temperature-Regulating Uniforms, to children like Sumati. These are less effective and prone to malfunction.

The difference in protection is a sign of broader adaptation inequality: some people survive comfortably and others sweat through systemic neglect. The suits signify status and climate privilege. In the novel they help the reader see everyday adaptation, and their technical basis draws on real-world prototypes such as MIT’s thermal comfort wearables. When Sumati wears her suit on the Hyper-Express, its limitations show at once, which highlights the difference between adaptation as performance and adaptation as justice.

The suit is both a shield and a symbol. It protects the wearer, and it also shows how institutions absolve themselves by issuing tools in place of building systems of care.
