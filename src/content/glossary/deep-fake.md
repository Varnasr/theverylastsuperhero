A deep fake is a digitally manipulated video or audio file designed to simulate real people convincingly, showing them saying or doing things they never did. By 2075, deep fakes will have evolved into high-resolution, real-time simulations powered by generative adversarial networks (GANs) and memory-mining AI. Governments, corporations and resistance groups all use them, for misinformation, protective deception or strategic narrative control. In The Very Last Superhero, deep fakes are part of the media environment Sumati grows up in: school broadcasts are routinely “corrected,” tribunal footage is contested and even family videos are suspect.

One segment of the Fallen Heroes Museum includes a side-by-side display of a Brigade speech and its falsified clone, and visitors are challenged to tell them apart. Meera once warned Sumati that “truth is now a function of repetition, not record.” Deep fakes blur the line between memory and manipulation, and they serve both propaganda and survival. Some resistance archivists specialise in “authenticity forensics,” which tries to verify origins using pre-disclosure hashing methods, though most such systems have been compromised.

In a world where seeing is no longer believing, the deep fake is a quiet weapon that replaces reality so convincingly that no one argues.
