---
term: "Company-Locked Comms"
section: "Surveillance Tools and Public Infrastructure"
order: 10
---

Company-locked comms are mobile communication devices tied to an individual’s ration card or employer ID. They cannot make unsanctioned outbound calls and restrict internet access, contact lists, and location services. In the story, they appear as tools of control and symbols of modern dependency—devices that appear functional but offer no real agency. Sumati’s inability to contact help after her father disappears stems directly from this system: the train terminal phone rejects her call, her device is deactivated, and strangers’ phones are similarly locked. These comms are emblematic of how information is filtered through trust scoring. The devices were introduced during the IKS curriculum reforms as “tools for civic safety,” but in practice, they monitor more than they enable. Real-world parallels include corporate-managed smartphones, school-issued tablets with blocked browsers, and employer-controlled work apps. In The Very Last Superhero, their presence shows how even the most mundane objects—phones, kiosks, uniforms—can enforce ideological conformity. The Resistance deliberately avoids these devices, using feature phones and analogue tools instead. Company-locked comms are not just useless in crisis; they are actively harmful, reinforcing a world where emergency communication is a privilege.
