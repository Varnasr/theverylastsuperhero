---
term: "Company-locked comms"
section: "Surveillance tools and public infrastructure"
order: 10
---
Company-locked comms are mobile communication devices tied to an individual’s ration card or employer ID. They cannot make unsanctioned outbound calls, and they restrict internet access, contact lists and location services. In the story they appear as tools of control and as symbols of modern dependency: devices that look functional and offer no real agency. Sumati cannot contact help after her father disappears because of this system. The train terminal phone rejects her call, her own device is deactivated, and strangers’ phones are similarly locked.

These comms show how information is filtered through trust scoring. They were introduced during the IKS curriculum reforms as “tools for civic safety,” and in practice they monitor more than they enable. Real-world parallels include corporate-managed smartphones, school-issued tablets with blocked browsers and employer-controlled work apps. In The Very Last Superhero their presence shows how even the most mundane objects, such as phones, kiosks and uniforms, can enforce ideological conformity.

The Resistance deliberately avoids these devices and uses feature phones and analogue tools instead. Company-locked comms are useless in a crisis and also actively harmful, because they reinforce a world where emergency communication is a privilege.
