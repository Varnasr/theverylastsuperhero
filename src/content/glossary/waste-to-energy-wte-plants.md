Waste-to-energy (WTE) plants are industrial facilities that burn municipal solid waste to generate electricity. They were promoted as climate-friendly solutions to India’s urban waste problem, and they became widespread after 2040. In reality they are more toxic: many of these plants emit harmful pollutants such as dioxins and furans, contaminate groundwater and displace nearby communities. The Okhla WTE plant in Delhi, which the novel references directly, was a flashpoint for public protests in the 2020s.

In the story, Rohan Kapoor works as a systems engineer at a WTE facility and believes he is contributing to climate solutions. His role becomes morally complex when Sumati discovers the plant’s darker truths: illegal detentions, toxic emissions and complicity in state violence. WTE plants in the novel represent the danger of technocratic hubris, the belief that any complex social or environmental problem can be burned away for profit.

They also reflect how infrastructure can mask dispossession. Rohan’s arc, bound up in this flawed technology, stands as a caution against blind faith in engineered “solutions” that ignore justice.
