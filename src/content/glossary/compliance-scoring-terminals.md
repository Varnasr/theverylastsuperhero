term: "Compliance Scoring Terminals"
section: "AI Systems and Surveillance Technologies"
order: 8
---

These kiosks are publicly placed devices that update, display, and enforce an individual’s Obedience Index score. Found in transit stations, ration shops, and even schools, they serve as both infrastructure and intimidation. In The Very Last Superhero, these terminals physically gatekeep access to essentials—Sumati is denied entry to public services, while others in line perform compliance rituals to raise their scores. Some terminals record mood, body language, and even facial micro-expressions to detect “latent noncooperation.” The terminals often appear innocuous: sleek, well-lit, and robotic voiced. But they represent how control is embedded into the everyday. The Resistance graffiti on some terminals—hacked messages, memory fragments, corrupted images—suggests they’ve been sites of both oppression and rebellion. Their presence critiques how behavioural tech shifts accountability from systems to individuals, creating a world where survival requires performance rather than honesty. In real-world terms, they’re not far off from biometric kiosks, AI proctoring systems, or facial recognition gates. In the novel, they form the algorithmic fences enclosing Sumati’s journey, the first signs that tech alone cannot hold back memory.
