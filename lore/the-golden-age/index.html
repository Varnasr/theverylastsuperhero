The Golden Age of Heroes

Twenty years when plasma-suited vigilantes, genetically enhanced defenders and state-funded climate responders dominated global headlines. It ended in a tribunal.

Twenty years of it. Plasma-suited vigilantes, genetically enhanced defenders, state-funded climate responders — all of them dominating global headlines.

The achievements were real, and that is the difficult part.

The Quantum Corps diverted three cyclones from Mumbai. The Delhi Climate Brigade built a micro-climate shield that protected the capital from the worst heat waves. The legendary Captain Indra single-handedly evacuated thousands during the 2055 Bangladesh flooding, when the angry Teesta turned eastern India into a marshy wasteland.

Every one of those sentences is true. Every one of them is also the setup for what the analysis found afterwards: the cyclone diversions manufactured the droughts, and the shields masked rising temperatures rather than addressing their causes, accelerating the crisis they promised to solve.

But they were trying to help, weren’t they?

That’s what most of them believed. But intention doesn’t protect you from misinformation.

Children are taught it now in a museum, walking past holographic biospheres and simulations of an Earth that is no longer there, and the lesson their teacher lands on at the abandoned Brigade headquarters is short: that’s why we don’t have superheroes anymore.
