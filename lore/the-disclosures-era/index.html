The Age of Heroes

For twenty years heroes soared in plasma suits and gravity-defying capes, and India worshipped them. The 2060 Disclosures ended it.

For two decades they soared across the skies in plasma suits and gravity-defying capes, diverting cyclones, shielding cities, evacuating thousands from rising floodwaters. Schoolchildren collected their holograms and traded cards. Ministers competed for photographs beside them.

Nobody asked the difficult questions. Nobody looked too closely at the corporate logos discreetly embroidered on the suits. Nobody wondered why, despite the miraculous interventions, the fundamental problems only worsened.

Then the tribunals. Statues toppled, murals defaced, and in their place new memorials:

Never Again Shall We Surrender Our Power to False Saviours

Climate education changed with them. Where children had once learned about heroic interventions, they now studied how the superhero phenomenon delayed meaningful climate action for decades, diverting resources from unglamorous but effective community-based solutions.

The archive holds no human account of this — only the transmission. Record one.
